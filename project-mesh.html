Emergency Communications · Systems · Field Testing

MESH USF

A SCOPE × ACM × USF CERT collaboration focused on resilient, low-barrier communications for volunteer emergency-response teams.

MESH USF Project LeadSCOPE Engineering DirectorStakeholder InterviewsMesh Networking3D Printing

Starting with the user problem

The project began by working with USF CERT as the stakeholder. I helped organize the interview logistics, including scheduling and room coordination, and we prepared the student team before meeting with CERT members.

We also held a small interview-preparation workshop for interested members. We provided question-formulation resources and ran mock interviews so the team could ask useful questions instead of jumping straight into a technical solution.

The interviews highlighted a core problem: during emergencies, cellular and power infrastructure can become unreliable or overloaded, while volunteer teams still need a practical way to coordinate.

Mesh-radio concept

The solution was a mesh communication network in which messages can move from node to node. Two distant radios may be outside direct range, but intermediate nodes can relay the message across the network.

The software team adapted an existing phone-based interface around the communication needs identified in the CERT interviews. On the hardware side, we prepared physical radios for deployment and testing.

Why mesh: the system does not require every device to communicate directly with every other device. Intermediate nodes can extend the practical communication path.

Hardware packaging and range testing

I helped with the physical radio nodes by 3D printing enclosures, preparing units for distribution, and participating in range testing. Testing involved separating radios until direct communication was lost, then using additional nodes between them to evaluate the multi-hop behavior.

Project leadership

I led the project from the SCOPE side while serving as SCOPE Engineering Director, coordinating with technical leads and helping connect stakeholder needs to the software, hardware, and testing work.

The project structure covered research and architecture, interface development, hardware deployment, field testing, iteration, and final documentation.
